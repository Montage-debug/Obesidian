[0] SC-RRT: Bidirectional Path Planning with Adaptive Dual-Superellipsoid Sampling
[1]  Yan Wu, Xingyu Guo1, Muchun Fan1, Feng Hong1, Hongyu Gao1, Jianyu Zheng1
[2] Abstract: 基于采样的路径规划算法在障碍物密集环境中常存在无效采样过多、搜索效率不足及可行路径获取速度较慢等问题。本文提出一种具有自适应双超椭球约束采样机制的双向快速扩展随机树方法。该方法采用自适应双超椭球约束采样（ADCS）策略，通过一个动态更新的中间参考点来定义两个采样区域，并利用基于搜索状态反馈的在线调节（SSFOR）机制，根据各搜索方向近期最优路径代价的改善程度构造反馈偏差，在搜索停滞时扩大搜索范围实现探索与开发之间的动态平衡。实验与物种基于采样的基线算法进行对比评估，结果表明, SC-RRT 在二维和三维场景中均成功找到了可行解，相较于 Dynamic-RRT，SC-RRT 平均路径长度缩短 12.6%，路径效率达到0.9553；其规划时间仅为 Informed RRT* 的 16.2%，采样节点数量降低 94.2%。表明该方法在保证可观路径质量的同时计算效率具有显著提升。此外，基于六自由度机械臂，在 RGB-D 感知构建的非结构化三维环境中开展实物实验。SC-RRT 规划得到长度 3.249 m 的多目标连续轨迹，机械臂实际运行路径长度为 3.245 m，路径长度偏差仅 0.12%；工具中心点（TCP）平均跟踪误差 1.98 mm，最大跟踪误差 3.09 mm，验证了规划轨迹能够在实体机器人系统中可靠执行。综上，SC-RRT 在路径质量与计算效率之间取得了较好的平衡，并表现出一定的真实机器人应用潜力。

[6] Introduction
[7] 随着工业机器人与服务机器人技术的快速发展，机器人正被广泛部署于复杂环境中，对其运动规划的可靠性与效率提出了更高要求。其应用场景已从结构化制造环境扩展至人机协作、非结构化空间以及空间受限操作等复杂任务环境中[1–5]。在实际应用中，规划算法不仅需要具备较高的计算效率，还需保证路径质量。然而，在障碍物随机分布或几何约束复杂的环境中，可行采样空间显著收缩，树结构扩展受限，从而使得算法难以同时实现快速收敛与高质量路径生成[6–10]。
[8] RRT、RRT-Connect 和 RRT 为基于采样的规划提供了成熟的框架[11–15]。它们各自不同的探索与优化策略，提供了求解速度与路径质量间的不同平衡[16–19]。为提升采样效率与搜索引导能力，国内外研究者提出了多种基于方向性采样与双向搜索增强的改进方法。诸如 ARRT-Connect、ABQ-RRT 以及几何引导采样等扩展方法，通过利用搜索树的演化信息或周围环境信息来改善搜索引导[20-22]。
[9] Informed RRT*将采样限制在一个能够改进现有解的椭球子集内[23,24]，为代价引导的约束采样提供了重要基础。而BIT*则将启发式采样与隐式随机几何图上的启发式搜索相结合，以提升高维空间中的规划效率[25,26]。后续方法通过自适应采样和路径优化进一步拓展了这些思路[27,29]。其中，Zhao等提出的Dynamic-RRT[29]引入基于超椭球体的启发式约束采样机制，通过估计路径代价确定采样区域，并结合Pareto支配节点选择策略[30]及问题分解方法，在保证路径质量的同时提升了可行路径生成效率。
[10] 这些研究促使人们进一步探讨采样区域应如何响应搜索进展，但仍存在两个关键局限性。过大的采样区域有利于探索，但可能将计算资源浪费在偏离有用路径的方向上；而过于受限的区域则可能限制替代连接的发现。本文考察了方向特定的采样几何与基于近期解成本改善的反馈机制相结合的方法。
[11] 针对上述问题，本文提出了SC-RRT，并引入自适应双超椭球约束采样机制（ADCS）。在双向搜索框架下，围绕一个共享的中间参考点构建两个椭球采样区域，将全局规划问题分解为两个协同子问题，从而为双侧独立约束采样提供结构基础。提升采样效率并保持必要的探索能力。此外，进一步提出基于搜索状态反馈的在线调节机制（SSFOR），利用当前最优完整路径的近期改善情况，对超椭球扩张因子及启发式采样概率进行自适应调整。在搜索初期或陷入停滞阶段，适当放宽约束以扩大搜索范围；在路径持续优化阶段，则逐步收紧约束以提高有效采样密度并抑制冗余扩展。该整体框架在二维和三维基准测试以及物理机械臂执行案例中进行了评估，以验证方法的实际可用性与工程适应性。
[12] The main contributions are as follows:
[13] 1. A bidirectional planning framework that uses a dynamic intermediate reference point to construct direction-specific ellipsoidal sampling regions.
[14] 2. A search-state feedback mechanism that jointly regulates region expansion and sampling probability using the recent improvement of the incumbent path.
[15] 3. An evaluation of overall planning performance in two and three dimensions, together with a physical case demonstrating the execution of SC-RRT-generated TCP paths on a manipulator.

[23] Adaptive Dual-Ellipsoid Constrained Sampling
[24] This paper proposes an Adaptive Asymmetric Dual-Superellipsoid Constrained Sampling (ADCS) strategy, as illustrated in Fig. 2, to adaptively regulate the sampling domains during bidirectional tree expansion. The two search directions are indexed by i∈{A,B}, where A and B denote the start-side and goal-side searches, respectively. For each search direction, the corresponding constrained sampling region is constructed according to its current search state. ADCS determines the geometric form and direction-specific location of the constrained sampling regions, whereas the SSFOR mechanism introduced in Section 2.3 dynamically regulates their constraint strength and informed sampling probability. [26] where γi denotes the expansion coefficient for search direction i, pi denotes its informed sampling probability,  is the current best estimated path cost for search direction i, and f1,i and f2,i denote the corresponding focal points. For the start-side search, f1,A = xstart and f2,A = xmeet, whereas for the goal-side search, f1,B = xgoal and f2,B = xmeet.
[27] The two constrained regions are constructed independently, forming an asymmetric constraint structure due to their different focal points and direction-specific path costs. During each iteration, informed sampling is performed within the corresponding constrained region with probability pi, biased sampling is performed toward a Pareto-optimal node selected from the opposite tree according to the Paretodominance principle [30], and global uniform sampling is used otherwise. This hybrid strategy balances focused search and global exploration.
[28] For each newly expanded node, a direct connection to the opposite tree is first attempted. If the distance exceeds the predefined threshold, the opposite tree greedily advances toward the newly generated node. Once the two trees are successfully connected, path generation is completed, followed by the same path-smoothing procedure for all compared algorithms [33,34] to obtain the final feasible trajectory.

[31] Search State Feedback-Based Online Regulation (SSFOR) Mechanism
[32] The effectiveness of the ADCS strategy is strongly influenced by the selection of the expansion coefficient γi and the informed sampling probability pi. To address this issue, a Search State Feedback-Based Online Regulation (SSFOR) mechanism is proposed to dynamically regulate these parameters according to the search progress, as illustrated in Fig.3.

Since the two search directions may exhibit different search behaviors, the feedback signal is calculated independently for each direction.

[39] Through this feedback regulation mechanism, the constraint strength of the ADCS strategy can be dynamically matched with different search stages. During the early exploration stage, relatively relaxed constraints maintain sufficient search coverage. After feasible solutions are obtained, gradually strengthened constraints improve sampling efficiency by concentrating samples in promising regions.
[40] To ensure the boundedness and reproducibility of the regulation process, γi and are restricted within predefined ranges. The expansion coefficient is bounded by γi ∈ [1.0, 4.0]. A larger γi expands the constrained sampling region and improves exploration capability, whereas a smaller γi produces a tighter sampling region and promotes local convergence. The informed sampling probability is bounded by pi ∈ [0.2, 0.95]. During the feedback regulation stage, pi is adjusted within this range according to the search state. The value pi=0 is used only before a feasible solution is obtained. A smaller pi maintains a higher proportion of global exploration, whereas a larger pi increases the contribution of informed sampling and promotes local refinement.
[41] Under the closed-loop regulation of SSFOR, both parameters are dynamically adjusted for each search direction within the above ranges, allowing the two constrained sampling regions to adapt independently to their respective search states. This regulation prevents excessive expansion or premature contraction of the sampling domains while maintaining a balance between exploration capability and convergence efficiency.

[48] Comparative Evaluation in Benchmark Environments
[49] To systematically evaluate the performance of the proposed SCRRT algorithm, comparative simulations were carried out in twodimensional and threedimensional obstacle environments against representative samplingbased pathplanning algorithms in terms of path quality and computational efficiency.
[50] Two workspaces of different dimensions were established, including a twodimensional workspace and a threedimensional workspace. Obstacle layouts, startgoal states, and maximum iteration budget were identical across all tested algorithms to guarantee fair experimental conditions. All algorithms shared a maximum iteration budget of 5000. The search terminated upon finding the first collision-free path or exhausting the iteration limit. This unified stopping criterion provides a consistent basis for comparing planning efficiency and path quality. Detailed environment parameters are listed in Table 1. Four widelyused metrics were adopted for performance assessment: Path Length, Planning Time, Node Count, and Path Efficiency. Path Length is the cumulative Euclidean distance between successive nodes on the output path, characterizing solution quality. Planning Time refers to the wallclock time consumed by the search procedure. Node Count counts all sampled nodes produced during planning for samplingefficiency assessment. [161] Conclusions
[162] In this study, a Superellipsoid-Constrained Bidirectional RRT (SC-RRT) algorithm was proposed for efficient motion planning in complex robotic environments. The method integrates bidirectional search, Adaptive Dual-SuperEllipsoid Constrained Sampling (ADCS), and Search State Feedback-Based Online Regulation (SSFOR). By constructing direction-specific asymmetric sampling regions and dynamically regulating their constraint strength according to the search state, SC-RRT reduces redundant exploration while maintaining sufficient search coverage. Comparative experiments were conducted in two-dimensional and three-dimensional Cartesian workspaces using RRT, RRT-Connect, RRT*, Informed RRT*, and Dynamic-RRT as baseline methods. Quantitative results in the two-dimensional environment showed that SC-RRT achieved a favorable balance between path quality and computational efficiency. Compared with Dynamic-RRT, SC-RRT reduced the average path length by 12.6%, while requiring 83.8% less planning time and 94.2% fewer sampled nodes than Informed RRT*. Although RRT* and Informed RRT* produced slightly shorter paths, SC-RRT maintained competitive path efficiency with substantially lower computational cost. The three-dimensional experiment further demonstrated the applicability of the proposed method to higher-dimensional planning environments.
[163] The practical applicability of SC-RRT was further evaluated on a six-degree-of-freedom robotic manipulation platform equipped with an RGB-D perception system. In an unstructured three-dimensional environment, the proposed method successfully generated and executed a continuous multi-target trajectory. The planned trajectory length was 3.249 m, while the actual executed trajectory length was 3.245 m, corresponding to a difference of 0.12%. The mean and maximum TCP tracking errors were 1.98 mm and 3.09 mm, respectively. These results indicate that the generated trajectory can be successfully transferred to the physical robotic system with satisfactory execution accuracy. Although real-world experiments introduced perception uncertainty, geometric reconstruction errors, and robot execution deviations, the proposed method maintained feasible execution performance in the real-world experiment.
[164] Overall, SC-RRT provides an adaptive sampling-based planning framework that improves sampling efficiency while preserving competitive path quality. The simulation and real-robot results demonstrate its potential for robotic motion planning in complex three-dimensional environments. Future work will focus on improving adaptability to dynamically changing environments, incorporating dynamic obstacle avoidance, and extending the framework to more complex manipulation tasks involving real-time perception and physical interaction.
